# Ensemble Action Production

This skill owns the production-level chain:

`original premise → causal sequence engine → ensemble matrix → music master → asset bibles → shot plan → local generation → semantic verification → edit → final acceptance`

It does not replace specialist skills. It coordinates them and rejects missing handoffs.

## Non-negotiable local boundary

- Use locally installed models only for media generation.
- Do not use Seedance, Higgsfield, hosted partner nodes, remote image/video APIs, or uploaded third-party generation services.
- Krea 2 owns still assets and keyframes.
- MiniMax Music 3 owns the original song.
- MiniMax H3 FL2VA/REF2VA owns video.
- Queue one GPU1 family at a time through `creative-model-phase`.
- Do not silently substitute models or precision tiers.

## Ubiquitous language

- **Story goal** — the fixed result the protagonist/team pursues across the proof.
- **Sequence jeopardy** — a circumstance that threatens a required stage of the story goal.
- **Tactical probe** — a reasonable action under incomplete information whose outcome changes the next move.
- **Turn** — an outcome that defeats expectation and changes the available path.
- **Audience reappraisal** — the before/after judgment of a character caused by the turn.
- **Ensemble matrix** — coverage proving every member has a distinct personality beat and capability beat.
- **Capability beat** — a visible demonstration of a member's unique operational contribution.
- **Music master** — the accepted MiniMax Music 3 mix that becomes the timing authority.
- **Beat map** — detected musical sections, beats, downbeats, and energy changes.
- **Shot unit** — one short H3 generation with one dramatic job, one primary action, and one camera behavior.
- **Acceptance ledger** — the evidence-backed PASS/REJECT/UNVERIFIABLE record for every asset and shot.

## Stage 0 — originality and rights gate

Before development:

- create original names, character identities, lyrics, logos, silhouettes, mech topology, alien biology, and world logic;
- describe genre and production qualities without naming living artists as imitation targets;
- keep reference-video prompts and unlicensed skills as research evidence only;
- record every model, source revision, license, and asset authorization;
- reject any concept whose recognizability depends on copying another property's characters, title, costume language, or signature scene.

## Stage 1 — causal sequence engine

Every scene must advance a causal chain toward the story goal.

| Sequence | Story-goal stage | Jeopardy and scale | Tactical probe | Information returned | Turn | Audience reappraisal | Resulting next move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules:

1. Removing a scene must break the causal chain or intentionally change rhythm; otherwise cut it.
2. Jeopardy threatens success; inconvenience alone is not enough.
3. A failed tactic must return information or alter state. Repeated zero-information attempts are filler.
4. Every resolved sequence needs a turn.
5. A turn without a character reappraisal or meaningful state change is inert.
6. Turns across the proof should deepen the audience's model of the team, not alternate randomly.

## Stage 2 — ensemble matrix

Each core member receives two distinct introductions:

1. **Personality beat** — ordinary behavior under social pressure.
2. **Capability beat** — their unique contribution under operational pressure.

| Member | Public role | Private pressure | Personality beat | Capability | Capability beat | Visual motif | Mech/vehicle mapping |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Reject the plan if:

- two members perform the same dramatic or tactical function;
- a member exists only as visual decoration;
- capability is asserted in dialogue but not shown;
- the leader receives all decisions and consequences;
- group coverage makes individual identities unverifiable.

Prefer four core members for the first local proof. Keep most H3 shots to one to three readable faces. A full-group wide is brief and must start from an accepted group keyframe.

## Stage 3 — music first

Generate an original 60–90 second proof track before choreography.

Music 3 caption contract:

- **Global metadata:** genre family, actual target BPM range, key/mode, energy arc, production texture, use case.
- **Vocal details:** lead timbre, group responses, harmony density, delivery, language plan.
- **Arrangement:** rhythm section, bass, synth/instrument palette, section-by-section development, transition design.
- **Lyrics:** original text using structural section tags.

Do not promise member-specific voices without qualification. Start with one stable lead identity plus layered group responses.

After generation, detect the **actual** beat grid. Requested BPM is intent, not evidence.

Load `references/music-and-shot-map-template.md` and record:

- intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus, bridge, breakdown, outro;
- downbeats and bar boundaries;
- vocal entries;
- impact accents;
- energy changes;
- planned shot boundaries.

### Local audio-conditioned H3 path

H3 REF2VA can accept audio guides. Use only accepted, consented, locally generated song excerpts of at most the qualified workflow duration. For performance qualification:

1. cut a 4–15 second excerpt on exact bar boundaries;
2. use one approved identity/group keyframe;
3. queue the dedicated BF16 REF2VA singing or audio-sync workflow;
4. verify identity, formation, mouth behavior, choreography, and audio alignment;
5. retain the Music 3 master as editorial authority;
6. reject drift rather than claiming sync from the prompt.

If the audio-reference path fails, use music-blind H3 movement and edit visually compatible portions to the detected beat map. Do not claim exact lip-sync.

## Stage 4 — asset bibles

No video generation begins before acceptance of:

- one accepted Krea face lock per member;
- one user-accepted FLUX.2 Klein BF16 Realism B per visible principal, qualified through `identity-realism-production` and recorded as the H3 production face authority;
- personless wardrobe sources and full-look screen tests;
- one-visible-face identity sheets whose sole face resolves to that accepted Realism B;
- group palette and formation sheet;
- one mech/vehicle bible per member;
- cockpit and exterior correspondence;
- weapons, controls, and scale evidence;
- alien species sheets and scale comparisons;
- clean location plates;
- original logo/text assets rendered deterministically, not by video diffusion.

Use `identity-realism-production` for mandatory production face finishing and H3 identity handoff. Krea-only faces may drive previews but are barred from final H3 production. Use `prop-continuity` for mechs, weapons, controls, hatches, and mating interfaces. Use `blocking-continuity` for formations, cockpit geography, and fight axes.

## Stage 5 — shot units

Each H3 shot unit must state:

- dramatic job;
- start state;
- one primary visible action;
- one performance task;
- one camera behavior;
- end state;
- required proof moments;
- forbidden additions and repeated actions;
- H3 family and exact references;
- music section/bar range, when applicable.

Do not ask one generation to perform exterior combat, transform into a cockpit, deliver dialogue, fire a weapon, and react. Split those into causal shots.

Any fight, fall, throw, impact, vehicle maneuver, collision, recoil, jump, landing, destruction event, or contact-rich prop action must pass through `action-physics-production` before the official H3 formatting pass.

### Action grammar

For impacts, write and verify:

`preparation → force transfer → target response → environment response → stabilization/aftermath`

An action is not accepted if only the wind-up is visible, contact is hidden, or the end state cannot be read.

### Cockpit/exterior rule

Do not show an exterior pilot and the same pilot inside a transparent cockpit simultaneously unless the geometry is explicitly qualified. Prefer:

1. exterior mech action;
2. motivated cockpit cut;
3. pilot response/action;
4. exterior consequence.

## Stage 6 — serialized execution

Use only these canonical families:

```bash
creative-model-phase prepare music3
creative-model-phase prepare krea
creative-model-phase prepare h3-fl2va
creative-model-phase prepare h3-ref2va
creative-model-phase release
```

Never call prepare or release while a graph is running or pending. Batch accepted work by family to avoid repeated model swaps.

## Stage 7 — acceptance ledger

Every accepted shot requires:

- prompt, seed, workflow, exact model selectors, dimensions, frames, and references;
- complete decode evidence;
- at least nine chronological full-frame samples;
- face, formation, prop, and contact crops where relevant;
- scene-function and action-occupancy verdicts;
- performance verdict from `performance-direction`;
- spatial verdict from `blocking-continuity`;
- prop/mech verdict from `prop-continuity`;
- music synchronization evidence for performance shots;
- final `PASS`, `REJECT`, or `UNVERIFIABLE`.

All critical invariants must pass. Technical success does not waive semantic failure.

## Stage 8 — deterministic edit

- Cut on the detected music grid, not requested BPM.
- Generate member cockpit portraits separately and composite multi-panel layouts deterministically.
- Preserve the Music 3 master; do not concatenate conflicting H3 music beds.
- Use H3 foley selectively under the song only after checking rights, continuity, and levels.
- Keep text, titles, subtitles, and logos out of diffusion outputs; add them in post.
- Verify final duration, streams, frame count, audio level, complete decode, and SHA-256.

## Completion

The proof is complete only when:

- every member has accepted personality and capability coverage;
- every sequence advances the causal chain and contains a meaningful turn;
- the song, beat map, and edit agree;
- all identities, formations, mechs, props, and locations pass evidence gates;
- no rejected shot is presented as repaired;
- all artifacts and licenses are recorded;
- the final master passes technical and semantic QA.
