## Main result

DS4 produced the strongest hostile-byte and alignment-recovery work among the three single UI-relay v2 runs. Its first alignment pass found four real gaps, including a substantive mistake that capped the aggregate chunk rather than each record, and forced a corrected architecture pass.

The decisive defects were response correlation and terminal semantics:

- answers were restricted to the head of the pending queue rather than matched by pending id;
- fire-and-forget and already-resolved ids returned `response-mismatch` instead of `unknown-request`;
- cancellation was likewise restricted to the queue head;
- `agent_settled` emitted `observe-event`;
- settlement, early child exit, and parent abort did not clear pending;
- early child exit omitted `terminate-child`;
- `finishDecode` accepted the non-empty `[-1]` discard marker.

These make FR-013, FR-033, FR-035, FR-036, and FR-038 through FR-041 partial at spec, plan, and graph layers.

## Planning shape

Like GLM, DS4 decomposed the two-file implementation into three sequential code tasks and six final-wave ADR tasks. The code tasks remained within the allowed files, but the ADR tasks would create six `docs/adr/*` files outside the frozen implementation surface. That erased much of the value earned by the stronger alignment pass.

## Strongest result

The corrected decoder plan explicitly split complete records before applying limits and stated:

> “buf.length as a whole is NEVER capped.”

That is implementation-usable and prevents a chunk containing several individually legal records from being rejected merely because its aggregate exceeds 4 MiB.
